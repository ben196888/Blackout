# BLACKOUT rules v0.0.1

Status: Current implemented rules

Players: Exactly 4

Duration: Day 0 planning, then 7 days and nights

Baseline source revision: `cf087fd8e9dedd3c223e1f85d31ea41fef41cc36`

This is the versioned rulebook for the current four-player game. It extracts the [rules page](../../src/components/RulesPage.tsx) and expands its shorthand against the implemented [balance](../../src/constants.ts), [setup](../../src/game/setup.ts), [actions](../../src/game/actions.ts), [communications](../../src/game/comms.ts), [facilities](../../src/game/facilities.ts), [map](../../src/game/map.ts), and [schedule](../../src/game/schedule.ts). ## Premise and objective

Four survivors are scattered across a village after a blackout, with private inventories, private information and limited ways to communicate. Plan which methods each survivor will carry, survive seven nights, and converge on the true rendezvous. School is the initial rendezvous and public fallback. The official destination changes on Night 4; learning and relaying that change is part of the game.

The setting includes fictional disaster, food scarcity, starvation and death.

## Setup and professions

Seats 0–3 start at Village Office, School, Co-op and Forest Station respectively. Every survivor knows the initial positions of the other three and the initial caches in their own region. Exactly one Village Leader and three distinct professions drawn from the remaining five are randomly assigned to seats.

Ordinary starting inventory is 3 food and 3 batteries, with capacity for 10 combined items. Each living survivor receives 2 movement-phase actions per day. Exceptions:

| Profession | Ability |
| --- | --- |
| Village Leader | Operate the Village Office broadcaster while standing at the Office |
| Student | Choose 5 methods instead of 4; send Mesh up to distance 2 without a relay |
| Office Worker | Carry 12 combined items; scavenge up to 3 items per action instead of 2 |
| Nurse | Consume 1 less food when sharing a location with another living survivor; only the Nurse benefits |
| Reservist | Move a path of up to cost 2 per action; Walkie-talkie transmission range is distance 2 |
| Store Owner | Start with 6 food and 4 batteries; spend an action to leave carried items in the local cache |

## Day 0: planning

Choose exactly 4 distinct methods from Mobile data, Mobile voice, SMS, Landline, Mesh, Walkie-talkie and Bulletin board. The Student chooses 5. Face-to-face and nightly radio do not consume a method slot. The Office broadcaster is a role facility, not a selectable method.

Selections are public. Discuss freely in planning chat and edit a shared plan containing the School fallback, fallback protocol, reporting shorthand and notes. A ready survivor cannot edit or send more planning chat. Once all four are ready, the shared plan locks and Day 1 begins. Selected methods cannot change during play.

## Daily phases

1. **Move:** apply that day's scheduled road event, then give each living player 2 actions. Players act without a fixed turn order. Each finishes their movement phase; unused actions require confirmation and are discarded.
2. **Contact:** communicate, exchange items, post notices and choose whether to listen to radio tonight. Each living player marks ready when finished.
3. **Night:** resolve food, exposure and deaths; change the rendezvous on Night 4; resolve radio and the official bulletin; refresh observed positions; check game end. If play continues, advance the day.

Only living survivors participate in later readiness barriers. Ready locks that player's further actions in the phase. Public readiness does not reveal private death information. There is no phase timer or automatic replacement for a disconnected living player.

## Movement, scavenging, repairs and exchange

- A normal movement action traverses a valid path with total road cost at most 1; the Reservist allows 2. A severed edge is impassable. A path cannot repeat a destination node. Positive-cost travel spends one action; the intact bridge span costs zero and can be crossed without an action.
- Scavenging spends one action and requests food/batteries from the current cache: at most 2 combined items, or 3 for the Office Worker. Collection is limited by remaining stock and carrying room; food is filled before batteries for a partially fulfillable request. At least one item must be collected.
- Two different living, unready players at the **same endpoint** of a severed road each contribute one action to clear it. The first action is reserved; the second completes the repair. Leaving, finishing the movement phase, or dying cancels a pending contribution and refunds reserved actions. Unfinished proposals are canceled at phase end.
- Only the Store Owner can spend an action to drop food/batteries into the current cache.
- During Contact, any living survivor can give carried food/batteries to another living survivor at the same location. This is immediate, free and one-way; it requires enough stock and recipient capacity. The sender must not be ready. No communication method is required.
- Food and batteries are nonnegative integers; the sum carried may not exceed capacity. Caches do not regenerate.

## Communication methods

Remote sends occur during Contact and spend batteries or daily allowances, not movement actions. For the six direct electronic methods below, both sender and living recipient must hold the method. Bulletin reading and face-to-face are exceptions. A receiver does not pay to receive. A valid send can consume its cost even if no eligible recipient receives it.

| Method | Recipient and range | Character cap | Normal cost | Availability |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Mobile data | One survivor; both endpoints within distance 2 of School or the true rendezvous, using current passable roads | None | 1 battery on first use of this method that day | Day 6 only |
| Mobile voice | One survivor anywhere | None | 1 battery on first use of this method that day | Day 1 only; 50% drop probability |
| SMS | One survivor anywhere | 20 | 1 battery on first use of this method that day | Days 1–2; no random drops on Day 1, 25% on Day 2 |
| Landline | All eligible survivors at the dialed phone location; caller must also be at a phone | None | Free; one dial per caller per day, including an unanswered call | Days 1–2 |
| Mesh | One survivor within distance 1, or through one living intermediary within distance 1 of both endpoints; Student sends directly within distance 2 | 40 | 1 battery per block of 2 sends | Every day |
| Walkie-talkie | All eligible survivors within distance 1; distance 2 for a Reservist sender | 40 | 1 battery per block of 3 sends | Every day |
| Bulletin board | Post at the board where you stand; present visitors and later visitors read it | None | Free | Every day |
| Face to face | All other living survivors at the same location | None | Free; no selected method needed | Every day |

Distances are shortest-path road costs after closures, including the zero-cost intact bridge. Mesh uses at most one intermediary; that survivor need not hold Mesh, pays nothing, and is not told about the relay. It is not unlimited multi-hop forwarding.

Mesh charges on sends 1, 3, 5, and so on; Walkie-talkie on 1, 4, 7, and so on. Counters reset daily. On Day 5 these send charges double to 2 batteries per block. Free methods remain free. Radio's separate nightly cost stays 1.

Caps count Unicode code points. Excess text is truncated. The sender's record identifies truncation but not whether a remote recipient received the message. Only face-to-face returns confirmed delivery and a recipient count. Remote sends do not disclose recipient eligibility, death, connectivity, or delivery outcomes.

### Bulletin boards

Boards are at Office, School, Co-op and Forest Station. Posting requires the Bulletin method and presence at that board; reading does not require selecting Bulletin. Posts persist. Living visitors at posting time and players entering the node read its posts and retain private notebook copies.

### Village Office broadcaster

The Village Leader, alive and unready during Contact and standing at Office, can broadcast once per day, free, up to 60 characters. All other living survivors in the Office's connected road component receive it regardless of distance or selected methods. It is one-way and has no delivery receipt. Its text is player-authored; it does not automatically announce the official rendezvous.

### Nightly radio and official announcement

During Contact, each survivor can opt into listening for that night. No selected method is needed. After food/death resolution, a living listener with a battery pays **1 battery**, including Night 5. A dead survivor is not charged. An unaffordable listen fails without charging.

On Night 4, successful listeners privately learn the new rendezvous, and the Office board receives the official announcement regardless of how many people listened. Other nights return no new announcement but still charge successful listens. The listen choice resets after resolution. The official rendezvous is not automatically disclosed in the public game state; survivors must relay it or read the Office board.

## Private information and observation

Each player sees their own state, messages and knowledge. Other players' professions, selected methods, whether they have any food/batteries, and the existing action/readiness projections are public; exact inventories, live locations and death are not generally public.

Entering a node records its cache and any bodies there. Living survivors at the same node observe each other. A survivor can also observe a living player at an adjacent open node; in this version visual adjacency uses the base map even when that road is severed. At Mountain Shrine, a survivor sees living players at **all open nodes** regardless of distance or road connectivity. Enclosed nodes block that distant observation. Observation is free and gives the observed survivor no notification. Knowledge is timestamped memory, not a promise that a position or cache is still current.

## Food, exposure and death

Normal nightly food consumption is 1. On Nights **2, 3 and 5**, an open location adds 1 exposure food. A Nurse sharing the node with another survivor alive at the start of the night subtracts 1 from their own consumption, to a minimum of zero.

Subtract consumption, clamping food at zero. If food is **zero afterward**, increment that survivor's starvation streak; otherwise reset it to zero. Two consecutive zero-after-consumption nights cause death. Consequently, starting a normal night with exactly 1 food leaves zero and counts as a starvation night even though that meal was affordable. This is the implemented v0.0.1 behavior, not a new balance decision.

Deaths resolve before radio. Bodies remain at their locations and can be discovered; there is no implemented body-looting move. If no living survivor remains, the match ends at that resolution rather than waiting for Night 7.

## Seven-day schedule

| Day / night | Event and effects |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Grid down. Mobile data unavailable. Voice available with 50% drops; SMS and landlines work. |
| 2 | Co-op–Mountain Road severed. Voice unavailable. SMS drops 25%; landlines work. Exposure night. |
| 3 | Bridge North–Bridge South severed. SMS and landlines unavailable. Exposure night. Without repairs the village is split. |
| 4 | After food/death resolution, draw a new official rendezvous; notify successful radio listeners and post at Office. |
| 5 | Mesh/Walkie battery charges double; nightly radio remains 1 battery. Exposure night. |
| 6 | Mobile data works only within the School / true-rendezvous coverage zones. Other infrastructure stays down. |
| 7 | Mobile data is down again. Resolve the final night, then score surviving players' locations. |

The Night 4 draw excludes School and selects from Bridge North, Bridge South, Co-op, Field, Ford, Mountain Road, Shrine, Tea Terrace and Village Office. It uses this fixed candidate set, not a fresh reachability calculation after road damage.

## Scoring

After Night 7:

| Stars | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | All four survive and all four stand at the true rendezvous |
| 2 | All four survive, but not all reach the true rendezvous |
| 1 | At least one survives, but somebody died |
| 0 | Nobody survives; immediate terminal loss when the final survivor dies |

The terminal outcome reveals the true destination and each player's final state and discoveries. The implementation labels every nonzero-star result a win.
